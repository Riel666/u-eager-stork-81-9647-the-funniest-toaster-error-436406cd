One error, 109 characters: the toaster reports bread not detected, admits it toasted the air anyway, and asks its owner to insert bread or lower their expectations.

It reads like a real appliance message — an error code, a plain diagnosis, an instruction — and the humour is the toaster's deadpan professionalism about a job it performed on nothing. The last clause turns the blame around: the machine did its part, the owner's expectations are the faulty component.
